The cross-check that broke the first model

PathwayGross $/ha/yr Farmer at 50/50Implied developer share
Rice methane$34.1$17.1 -105% to 41%
Biochar$450.0$225.0 84% to 96%

Published smallholder pilots earn US$20–70 per hectare per year. A flat 50/50 split puts rice-methane farmers below that band and biochar farmers at roughly three times its top — so the same split cannot describe both.

The negative share is the finding. To pay a farmer $70/ha from rice methane, the developer would have to pay out more than the credit is worth: the whole credit generates $34/ha. Biochar's 84–96% is what a pyrolysis business looks like — the processor carries the equipment cost, so the credit cannot be split like a land-based one. Biochar is a processing business that sources from farms; rice methane is a farmer-aggregation business.

Swing in rice-methane developer revenue, one lever at a time. Enrolment dominates — and it has the least evidence behind it. Price and yield can be bounded from published data; enrolment cannot, because nobody has demonstrated it at scale in India. That is where diligence belongs.

Sow&Reap's 1.07 credits per farmer is the only bottom-up check available on the credit-yield assumption anywhere in this market — and it is credits per farmer, not per hectare. That one missing conversion is the weakest link in every sizing of this market, including this one.
